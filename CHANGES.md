# What's new in ClipEdge

## 2.3.3

- **The drawer shows its whole list as it opens.** Since long lists started building only the rows near what's on screen, opening the drawer could show just its first row until you searched or scrolled. It now builds every row it shows.
- **The search key is `/`.** In the drawer, press `/` (as in Gmail) to go to the search instead of F; ⌘F still works.
- **Text comes before Images.** The tabs read All, Text, Images, Other: Text is 1 (⌘1 in the window) and Images 2, the easier reach.
- **Every part of the storage bar names its size.** Each label shows its size and percent after its name, in a fainter tone (*Used · 438 GB | 88%*), and resting the pointer on the item count shows the same bar at any time.
- **The selected tab is readable in Light mode.** Its name and key were dark on the purple fill; they are now white there.
- **Keep far more history.** ClipEdge no longer stops at 50 items: it keeps as many as fit in 1% of the free space on your Mac's disk, letting the oldest go first (at most 20,000). When that 1% is under 1 GB, a 💾 with a ⚠️ appears before the item count; rest the pointer on it to see your disk as one bar (what is used, ClipEdge's share and what is free; the numbers are in the bar's tooltip) and what to do about it. The history now saves in a folder (`History.store`) that writes only what is new, so copying stays quick however long it grows; the first launch moves your history there and leaves the old `History.plist` in place. Long lists open and scroll quickly too.
- **A sample history, behind the Option key.** Hold Option in the ClipEdge menu: *Save History Now* becomes **Use Sample History**, a second history kept for screenshots and videos. The first switch fills it from your own history with up to six passages of text, four Figma copies, four links and four pictures (never files or app data). While it shows, copying something adds it, deleting removes it, and every change is kept; your own history is saved and left alone until you switch back. The menu bar icon turns into theater masks, and the menu's first item takes you back. In your own history, Option turns *Clear History* into **Add “…” to Sample History**, which copies the newest item across.
- **Make the drawer wider or narrower.** Drag its inner edge (either side edge when it sits at the top of the screen) left or right; a small grip shows while the pointer is on it. The drawer keeps the width you leave it at, from as narrow as its four tabs allow (Select then moves under the search, beside the count) to most of the screen. The tab itself isn't part of the edge.
- **Drag the tab to open or close the drawer.** Drag the tab away from its screen edge and the drawer follows the pointer; let go and it finishes opening or closing on its own, at the speed you let go with. A quick flick decides by its direction; a slow release by whether the drawer is past halfway. Dragging the tab along its edge still moves it, to another edge too.
- **Sizes in Settings.** A new *Sizes* section has three fields that work like the others: **Text size** enlarges everything ClipEdge shows, from 80% to 200% (the drawer keeps its width and fits more snugly; the Option–Command–\\ window and Settings grow with it); **Thumbnail size** makes the pictures at the start of each row in the drawer and the window larger or smaller, 50% to 300% (the rows grow to fit; the Images grid keeps its own slider); **Preview size** makes Quick Look larger or smaller, 50% to 200%, beside the drawer and at the pointer. Reset ⌥⌘R puts them back to 100% with the rest.
- **The drawer reads well whatever is behind it.** Its glass used to take its brightness from the window behind it, so in Dark mode the drawer's light text could sit on light glass, and in Light mode its dark text on dark glass. The drawer now lays the same ground as the Option–Command–\\ window under its content, a little see-through, in both modes.
- **Quit ClipEdge.** The menu item no longer says *Save History &*: quitting still saves your history first.
- **Nothing explains Esc any more.** The window's hint, the delete question (*⌘⌫ again deletes*), the preview ask's *Cancel* and *Back*, the update question's *No Thanks*, Quick Look's arrows line, the raw data's guide, the card info and the tooltips no longer mention it. Esc still steps back one thing at a time.
- **Every key shown after a word sits in a cap.** The window's buttons (*Paste* [⏎], *Copy* [⌘C] …), the selection's buttons, *Raw data* [⌘I], the preview ask, the raw data's *Copy* buttons and Settings' *Reset* show their keys in the same small darker-gray box as the tabs, and so do the hints: the search's placeholder, Quick Look's line under the drawer's count, its arrows, and the held magnet's *preview* key. Two keys for one action read *[⌫] or [⌘⌫]*. In the Option–Command–\\ window the hint now sits on its own short line under the buttons.
- **Text size reaches every panel.** Send to and the preview ask beside the drawer, the selection's panel, the card info, Quick Look's date, facts and arrows (and its raw data), and the held magnet's words now grow and shrink with *Text size* too. Quick Look's picture keeps the size *Preview size* gives it.
- **Each tab's key sits in its own gray cap.** The tabs read *All*, *Images*, *Text* and *Other*, each followed by its key in a small darker-gray rounded box (0–3 in the drawer, ⌘0–⌘3 in the Option–Command–\\ window), so "Images 1" no longer reads as one image. The tab bar is a little taller (28 points) to give the box room, and the drawer's **Select** shows its S the same way (⌘S in the window). VoiceOver reads each as its name, then its key: "Images, 1".
- **ClipEdge no longer takes your typing after you switch apps.** If you clicked back into the drawer from another app and then closed it, the collapsed tab could keep the keyboard, so what you typed next in that app went nowhere. The same could happen when Send to (or the preview ask) was open and you clicked another app; after answering the first-launch update question, with its buttons or from the Updates menu; and, with the drawer tab hidden, when the drawer closed at once (Control–Command–V, or Esc with Reduce Motion on). ClipEdge now leaves the keyboard with the app you are in; Esc and ← in Send to still bring it back to the drawer.
- **More keyboard layouts and clearer VoiceOver keys.** Search editing, Ask’s Copy prompt and Publish recognize the matching key positions on non-Latin layouts, while Latin letters continue to follow layouts such as Dvorak. Drawer buttons, card help, Publish and preview arrows speak shortcut names instead of key symbols.
- **Whole compact-row details and cleaner magnets.** Hover a compact history row for its complete text, facts and paths, even outside selecting mode. Small-magnet captions no longer paint square background blocks.

- **Publish keeps its corner.** In the Option–Command–\\ window and the drawer, the maintainer's Publish button now sits alone in the top-right corner: nothing above it and nothing to its right. In the drawer the Settings gear moves just left of it while it shows. Ordinary and released copies never show Publish, so their gear stays where it was.
- **Select several items instead of Delete all.** The window's *Delete all* (Shift–Command–Delete) and the drawer's *Clear…* are gone. Press **Select** (**S** in the drawer, **Command–S** in the window) and the cards slide right to show a circle each; a picture in the Images grid shows its circle in its corner. Click a card or its circle, or press Return, to select it; Shift–Up and Shift–Down select as they move; **A** in the drawer, **Shift–Command–A** in the window, selects all of them, or none when all are. Command-click a card to start selecting from it, and Shift-click to select everything between. Once anything is selected, a panel shows how many and lists their titles, with what you can do with them: **Copy as text** (one plain-text copy of them all, one per line), **Paste as text** (the window only), **Open in Preview** and **Delete**, which asks first: in the window a second Command–Delete deletes, and in the drawer an alert asks. Holding Command–Delete down never counts as the second press. In the window the panel takes the card's place; beside the drawer it opens outside its edge and leaves the keyboard with the cards. A search or another tab lets go of selections you can no longer see, so nothing hidden is ever deleted. Esc, or pressing Select again (now **Done**), stops selecting. The menu bar's *Clear History* still empties the whole history, asking first.
- **Maintainer only: long release notes publish.** Preparing a release whose notes ran past 12,000 characters finished its tests and build, then stopped with "The release helper's response is missing a valid changes." Notes may now be as long as a GitHub release allows (125,000 characters); longer ones are refused at once, before anything is tested or built, with a sentence saying how long they are. A reply missing a field now names it in words.
- **Turn the card over to see what was copied.** **Command–I** in the window (**I** on a drawer card, for its Quick Look) turns the card around to show the copy's raw data: when it was copied, its kind, size and fingerprint, then every format it carries (the page it came from, its HTML, its plain text, a browser's own tokens), each with its name, description and size. Text shows as text, never rendered, up to its first 64 KB; anything else shows its first 512 bytes in hex. Each format has a **Copy ⌥1**, **⌥2**… button: text copies as plain text, anything else as that format alone. Select part of a text and Command–C copies just that. **Command–T** (T in the drawer) moves the keyboard into the next text, where the arrows move and Shift selects, and Page Up and Page Down scroll. The card stays turned while you move to other items, so two copies can be compared; Command–I again, or Esc, turns it back, and every opening starts from the front. Reduce Motion replaces the turn with a fade.

- **Hide the tab and keep the drawer.** Settings now has *Hide drawer tab* with a nested *Keep drawer enabled*. Keep it enabled to open from the same screen-edge hover area, menu, or shortcut; turn it off to use Clipboard History and Quick Look without the drawer.
- **Show Clipboard stays open.** The menu now toggles Show / Hide Clipboard, with a configurable **Control–Option–Command–Space** shortcut in Settings. Opening by command holds the drawer open even while the pointer is elsewhere; hover-opened drawers keep their close grace.
- **A hovered drawer closes while Settings is open.** With Settings open, a drawer opened by hovering its tab (or the tab's place, when the tab is hidden) stayed open after the pointer left. It now closes after its usual grace, so you can watch the closing slide you are tuning. Opening Settings from the drawer, with its gear or by right-clicking its tab, keeps the drawer in view while Settings is open; once Settings closes, moving away closes it again. When a drawer that opened over Settings closes, Settings gets the keyboard back, so your next arrow or ⌘Z reaches the field you were tuning.
- **The drawer slides smoothly over 300 ms.** Text and tiles keep their size during the slide; quick toggles reverse it. Reduce Motion makes it immediate.
- **Tune the slide and both delays, field by field.** Settings has four number fields that work alike. Under *Open the drawer*, **Hover delay** (0–2 s) replaces its slider and is dimmed unless *Delayed hover* is chosen; under *Close the drawer*, **Maximum close delay** (0–3 s) replaces its slider; under *Drawer motion*, how long opening and closing each take (0–1500 ms; 0 is at once). Each delay reads like "0.35s", the unit right after the number, and both delays are dimmed while the drawer is off. Every slide starts and stops softly, as it always has; the slide you have is 300 ms, and nothing changes until you change it. Each field works three ways: drag its handle left or right, and the drawer takes each new value as you drag (letting go makes the drag one undo step); click or Tab into the number and it is selected, ready to type over (letters are ignored, so "300ms" is fine, and in a delay a comma works as the point, so "0,5" is half a second; Return keeps it, Esc puts the old number back and leaves the field, and a second Esc closes Settings); or press ↑ and ↓, with ⇧ for ten at a time (a step is 10 ms, or 0.05 s for a delay); an arrow steps from the number last kept, and puts it back in place of any number typed but not yet kept. A typed number is kept however you leave its field: Return, Tab, a press anywhere else in Settings (another field's handle and Reset included), another window taking the keyboard, or closing Settings. (A drawer that opens by hover while you type slides open with the number from before; the typed number is kept as it opens.) A dimmed field takes no drag, typing or undo, Tab passes it, and pointing at it says why it is dimmed. **Reset ⌥⌘R** puts all four back (0.35 s, 0.5 s, 300 ms and 300 ms). Hover or type in a field and **↺ ⌘Z** appears just above its top right once there is something to undo, with **↻ ⇧⌘Z** to its left once something was undone; neither ever moves the field or anything near it. While one field has the keyboard, only its buttons show, and ⌘Z is its; with no field holding the keyboard, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z reach the field under the pointer, and dragging a handle leaves no other field holding it, so the field you drag shows its buttons. Opening Settings again with the pointer where it closed shows that field's buttons on the pointer's next move. ⌘A, ⌘C, ⌘X and ⌘V work in the number, as in the search. While you type a number, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z, and the two buttons, undo and redo the typing first. An arrow press at the end of a field's range (↑ at 2 s for the hover delay, ↓ at 0 ms) changes nothing and records nothing, so the next press is its own undo step. Undo and redo are remembered only while ClipEdge runs, and only the current values are saved. A slide already under way finishes with the duration it started with, and Reduce Motion still skips the slide. The first press on a handle drags, even while the drawer has the keyboard. Settings is 60 points wider, so each field's label fits on one line beside its undo buttons. The chords for Reset, undo and redo cannot be changed yet.
- **Esc closes Settings from a checkbox or a menu.** Held down in a field, or while recording a shortcut, Esc leaves the field or stops recording without also closing Settings; the next press closes it.
- **Single keys in the drawer, as in Gmail.** While the drawer's cards hold the keyboard, **0**, **1**, **2** and **3** show All, Images, Text and Other, **F** goes to the search with its text selected (Command–F still works), and **,** opens Settings as the gear does, so the drawer stays open while Settings is. Each tab shows its key (All 0, Images 1, Text 2, Other 3), the search's hint ends with F, the gear's tooltip reads "Settings ← ," (the tab's, "Settings ← right-click"), and an item's info lists the three keys. The keypad's 0–3 work too; on keyboards without Latin letters (Russian, Greek, Hebrew) F is the key in F's place, and a comma typed with Shift still opens Settings. With any other modifier (Shift–1, Option–F and the like) these keys do nothing special, and in the search every character is text. A number key keeps the chosen item when the new tab still lists it, and scrolls it into view, so Return or Delete never acts on an item out of sight. The Option–Command–\\ window keeps Command–0 to Command–3 and Command–F: its search always has the keyboard there, so a bare 1 types a 1. VoiceOver no longer reads the drawer's keys on that window's rows.
- **Command shortcuts work on Russian, Greek and Hebrew keyboards.** On a layout without Latin letters, Command–F, Command–C and Command–O in the Option–Command–\\ window, Command–O in the drawer, and Option–Command–A in the maintainer copy did nothing. Each is now the key in that letter's place, as the drawer's F already was; on Latin layouts such as Dvorak, the key that types the letter still decides.
- **The window's search names its key.** The Option–Command–\\ window's search hint now ends with ⌘F, as the drawer's ends with F.
- **Esc in the Option–Command–\\ window clears the search's undo too.** If the keyboard had left the search (a click on the item card's text), Command–Z after Esc could replay the cleared typing and break undo in the search until ClipEdge restarted. The drawer already cleared it.
- **Only Command–O opens Preview from the drawer.** Option–Command–O and Control–Command–O opened it too; now the drawer takes exactly Command–O, as the Option–Command–\\ window does.
- **After a delete, the drawer chooses the next item.** Deleting an item in the drawer chose the newest; now, as in the Option–Command–\\ window, the item that moves into its place is chosen.
- **A delete that cannot be saved says so in the Option–Command–\\ window too.** There it only beeped; now it shows the drawer's message, "ClipEdge could not save the deletion."
- **One wording for deleting.** The drawer and the Option–Command–\\ window now ask the same, "Delete permanently?" for one item, with the same explanation.
- **A delete waits only on the item it asked about.** In the Option–Command–\\ window, after Command–Delete asked "Delete permanently?", moving the pointer onto another row and back took the question away, yet one more Command–Delete still deleted the item. Now choosing another row, with the pointer, an arrow or a tab, lets go of the question and the footer stops saying "esc keeps"; Command–Delete on the item asks again.
- **VoiceOver says which item a delete asks about.** In the Option–Command–\\ window, a row waiting for a second Command–Delete was read as "Delete permanently? ⌘⌫ again deletes · esc keeps", without the item's name and with the keys as symbols. It now reads the item's name, then "Delete permanently? Command-Delete again deletes, Escape keeps", the keys in words as the footer's help says them.
- **Cards moving under a still pointer leave the drawer's choice alone.** When the drawer opened, filtered or scrolled under a pointer that had not moved, the card that came under it took the selection from the keyboard. Now, as in the Option–Command–\\ window, hovering chooses a card only once the pointer moves.
- **The drawer deletes with Delete, Forward Delete or Command–Delete, nothing else.** Delete with Option, Shift or Control held (Option–Delete and the like) asked to delete the chosen item too.
- **The drawer's search clear button shows everything again.** Clicking ⓧ in the drawer's search emptied it but left the list filtered by the old search.
- **An Other tab.** Beside All, Images and Text, the drawer and the Option–Command–\\ window (where Command–3 shows it) now have **Other**: files from the Finder, Figma layers, copies in an app's own format (see the next note) and anything ClipEdge cannot read. Tabs can overlap: a copied text layer is under Text and Other, a Keynote slide under Images and Other, and All still shows everything. Searching, Quick Look's arrows, the count line and the drawer's tab rule (a copy that the tab you were on would hide opens the drawer on All) all cover the new tab.
- **Copies in an app's own format say which app.** A copy from Keynote, Notes, Sketch, Photoshop or any app with its own clipboard format used to show as "Clipboard item" with two type names. It now reads "iWork data", "Notes data" or "Sketch data" (named from the format itself), with the number of formats it carries, a line saying that it pastes back into the app it came from exactly as copied, and, in its card, Quick Look, Preview and info, every format listed in full. It lists under Other, search finds it by the app's name, "app data" or a format, and a plain-text paste (Control–Command–V) gives its name and formats instead of a beep. Return and Command–V still paste every byte back. A copy that carries only a browser's or the pasteboard's own notes stays "Clipboard item", and a declared text or picture format beside the text (Markdown, a calendar event, a property list) is one ClipEdge reads, not an app's own.
- **A picture inside an app's copy is the preview.** Copies that carry a picture beside the app's own data (a Keynote slide, a Sketch layer, a chart from Numbers) now show that picture everywhere a picture shows, under Images as well as Other, with the app's name and format count among their facts. Pictures in any format ClipEdge can decode count too (WebP, BMP, HEIC), not only PNG, TIFF, JPEG, GIF and PDF.
- **Quick Look shows more formats.** A copy that carries rich text previews formatted; one that carries JSON, Markdown, CSV, XML, a property list, source code or another declared format (an app's own document type included) previews in that format, drawn by the previewers already on your Mac. Plain text, HTML-only and Figma copies keep their text cards; a picture stays a picture, and an SVG now previews as pixels rather than through a web renderer (one that cannot be drawn shows its formats). The card in the Option–Command–\\ window and an item's info add a line such as "Quick Look shows its RTF format" when this applies. Command–O still opens pictures and PDFs in Preview and everything else as a page of text.
- **Maintainer only: Ask for a preview, Option–Command–A.** In the maintainer copy, the Option–Command–\\ window's footer and the drawer answer Option–Command–A for the selected item with a small form (one optional line, **Write brief ⌘⏎**, **Cancel esc**) that writes a self-contained brief for an agent into the vault at `projects/clipedge-preview-<name>/AGENTS.md` (`<name>` is the app whose own format the copy carries, else the copy's kind: text, image, file, figma or clipboard-item): the copy's formats and sizes, what ClipEdge shows today and where the code lives, never what was copied. The form's place then shows where the brief went, with **Open in Obsidian ⌘⏎** and **Copy prompt ⌥⌘C** (a copy ClipEdge itself never records); in the drawer, the form's buttons wrap onto a second row when they do not fit. A second ask about the same app, or about any other copy of the same kind, adds a dated request to the same project while it is open; once that project is completed or abandoned, a new dated brief is written beside it. Ordinary and released copies show nothing of this.

- **Every opening starts with an empty search.** The drawer and the Option–Command–\\ window used to come back to an earlier search, so something you had just copied could be hidden until you noticed the search and cleared it. Now each opening clears the search and selects the newest item shown. The Option–Command–\\ window opens on All. The drawer stays on the tab you last chose, unless something you copied since you last opened it would be hidden there (a picture on Text, text on Images, or Figma layers or a file on either); then it opens on All. Settings no longer has *Reopen on the last search*. Command–Z right after an opening no longer replays typing from before it, which could break undo in the search until ClipEdge restarted.
- **Figma layers say what they are.** Layers copied in Figma in Chrome (or another Chromium browser) used to show as "Clipboard item" with two browser type names, and nothing could preview them or paste them as plain text. They now read "1 Figma layer from *your file*" (or "5 Figma layers…", or "Figma layers cut from…" after a cut), with the editor and site beside them, and a line explaining that they paste back into Figma, editable. The file's name comes from Chrome's note of the page; without it they read "1 Figma layer". Return still pastes them into Figma exactly as copied. A plain-text paste (Control–Command–V, or Control–Command–Return in the history window) gives a link back to the layers instead, Send to can open that link in your browser, Quick Look shows the explanation and link (in dark mode too), and Command–O opens them as a page in Preview. Search finds them by file name, "Figma" or the editor. Copies of a variable or of copied properties read "Figma clipboard data", and a selection of hundreds of layers is recognized too.
- **Figma text stays text.** A copied text layer carries its words as well as the layer. It is titled by its words, lists under Text, pastes its words with Control–Command–V, and its words fill its card and Quick Look. Its facts say where it came from ("Figma text from *your file*"), and its card explains that Figma gets the editable layer, other apps get its text, and Send to › Open in opens a link to it.
- **Copies of only HTML, or of rich text that can hold pictures, show their text.** A copy that carried HTML, or rich text in the form that can hold pictures (RTFD), but no plain text was a blank "Clipboard item". Its words now title it, fill its card and preview, appear under Text, and paste with Control–Command–V, with any picture in it left out of the title and the paste; a copied color shows as a swatch, and a lone web address names its site and opens from Send to. A copy from a web page in Chrome or another Chromium browser names the site it came from, such as "from claude.ai", and never the page's full address; the site is shown, never pasted.
- **Dark mode looks right everywhere.** When your Mac is in dark mode, or switches to it while ClipEdge is running, the Option–Command–\\ window, its item card, the delete-all question, Quick Look, Send to, an item's info and the small cursor magnet now turn dark with it. Before, some of them stayed white, so their light text vanished and an item's card looked blank. The kind symbols in drawer and window rows show light on dark rows, and a dark color swatch keeps its outline.
- **Starting up no longer freezes on an unreachable file.** A copied file in iCloud or on a disconnected drive used to hold up launch while ClipEdge opened it. File items now show a type icon at once and load their preview in the background; images keep full resolution for search. (Found and fixed with ChatGPT on Mom's Mac.)
- **Publish deliberately from the development copy.** A bright Publish button in the drawer and keyboard window shows when that running build differs from the latest published release. The button prepares a fixed, tested release for review; publishing uploads that same build. It disappears only after the published content is verified to match. While it prepares or publishes, the button fills as each step finishes. This maintainer control does not appear in ordinary installations.
- **A failed release check stays visible.** Offline or failed checks show a neutral retry control. A published match describes the available release, not whether another Mac has installed it yet.

## 2.3

- **Turn the cursor magnets off, all or some.** Settings → *Cursor magnets*: one switch, and while it is on, *On copy*, *From the drawer* and *From the window*. With one off, a copy just joins the history and a pick goes straight onto the clipboard, with nothing following the pointer. Quick Look still opens.
- **An item's info no longer covers it.** Hover a card for 2 seconds and its info (text, facts and keys) opens next to the drawer, where Send to opens, instead of under the pointer.
- **The keyboard comes back to your app.** Closing the Option–Command–\\ window with Esc, Return or Command–C returns the keyboard to the app you were in, so your next keys and your own Command–V land there.
- **Editing the search works as usual:** select all, cut, copy, paste, undo and redo. (In the Option–Command–\\ window, Command–C still picks up the selected item.)
- **Colors show as swatches.** A copied color value (hex, RGB, HSL) shows as a circle of that color; it still pastes as the text you copied.

## 2.1

- **ClipEdge keeps itself up to date.** On first launch it asks once: "Keep ClipEdge up to date automatically?" If you say yes, it looks on GitHub once a day for a newer version, checks that the download is signed by the same developer as the copy you have, and installs it while you are not using ClipEdge. Nothing about you or your clipboard is sent. Your macOS permissions (Accessibility, Input Monitoring) carry over, so there is nothing to approve again. The ClipEdge menu bar icon → **Updates** turns this off, checks now, shows what changed, and goes back to the previous version.
- **Clipboard history by keyboard: Option–Command–\\.** A compact window opens over the app you are in, with the newest item selected. Type to search, press the arrow keys to choose, and Return to paste. Command–1, 2 and 3 show All, Images or Text.
- **It reopens where you left off.** Reopen the window or the drawer within a few minutes of using an item and your search is back, with that item selected. Settings → *Reopen on the last search* sets the minutes or turns it off.
- **Command–F goes to the search** in the drawer and in the history window.
- **Paste as plain text: Control–Command–V**, anywhere. In the history window, Control–Command–Return does the same for the selected item.
- **Send to: Tab.** Open the selected item in an app that can read it, or paste it into another running app.
- **Command-click pastes the item you are holding.** A plain click keeps holding it.
- **Facts on every item:** words and characters for text; pixel size, file size and type for pictures; size, type and full path for files; the site for links.

Moving from 2.0: install 2.1 once (see the README). From then on ClipEdge updates itself.

## 2.0

First public release: the clipboard drawer at the screen edge, with search, All / Images / Text tabs, Quick Look previews and the cursor magnet.
